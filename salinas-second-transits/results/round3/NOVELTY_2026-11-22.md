- "Literature" means a web search for each TIC ID with and without "TESS", plus the Salinas et al.

  - The paper's text names none of the four. It lists them only in its Tables 4 to 6.
  - It covers Sectors 27 to 60.

  inside a machine-readable table could have been missed.

**So none of the four periods has been reported anywhere we can see.** They come only from this repo:
rounds 2 and 3, plus the blind re-derivation in `independent/`.

## Context

- **Salinas et al. are following up their own singles.** The accepted RNAAS note (AAS82207) left out
  their earlier CTOIs (Salinas et al. 2025) for that reason. The corresponding author is H. Salinas
  (Universidad Adolfo Ibáñez). Co-author R. Brahm works on RV follow-up of TESS warm giants (the WINE collaboration).
- **230133616 is at ecliptic latitude +82.7°,** in the northern continuous viewing zone. That is why
  it has 37 transits in S16–S86. A 0.59%-deep periodic signal there with no TOI is unexpected. Possibly
  a pipeline vetting step rejected it as an EB, but we have found no record of that.

## The eclipsing neighbour of TIC 107113048

The blind run found a G = 12.74 eclipsing binary 97″ from 107113048 (Gaia DR3 3463196954429755904 =
TIC 107116357, T = 12.38). One of its eclipses overlaps the target's S90 transit.
- It is **not** in Gaia DR3 `vari_eclipsing_binary`.
- It is **not** in the TESS EB catalogue (Prša et al. 2022), within 10″.
- It is **not** in VSX, within 10″.
- It is **not** an ExoFOP TOI or CTOI.
- Gaia gives RUWE 0.99 and `non_single_star` = 0.
- Its eclipses are 7–18% deep in its own 3×3 pixels, with one in each of S37, S64, S90 and S101
  (`independent/out/eb_neighbour_107113048.txt`).

It looks like an uncatalogued eclipsing binary. Its period has not been worked out.
