0. SHORT ANSWER
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Both eclipses deepen monotonically:
  - primary: 6.2 % (2014, Kepler band) -> 20.1 % (2021) -> 27.0 % (2023) -> 34.5 % (2026);
  - secondary: absent in 2014 (< 0.08 %) -> 4.3 % (2021) -> 12.0 % (2023).
* One eccentric two-sphere model, with radii, surface-brightness ratio and e sin w shared across
  seasons and only i changing, fits every TESS eclipse to the noise (rms 0.8-1.4 ppt).
* Inclination per season: 85.77 (2014), 87.23 (2021), 87.86 (2023), 88.55 (2026) deg.
  - Statistical errors are 0.004-0.04 deg.
  - Systematic errors are 0.19-0.32 deg; they are mostly common to all seasons (third light,
    reduction).
* di/dt is +0.305 deg/yr (2021-23), +0.316 deg/yr (2023-26) and +0.197 deg/yr (2014-21).
  - The rate is not constant: it has accelerated since 2014.
  - A constant TESS-era rate would predict only a ~1 % primary in 2014, but 6.2 % was observed.
* The primary eclipse began about 2007-2010; it was certainly already in progress in 2014.
* The secondary eclipse began about 2018-2019.
* At the TESS-era rate:
  - the primary becomes total (star 1 fully hidden behind the larger star 2) in ~2029.5
    (range 2027.5-2031);
  - i reaches 90 deg in ~2030-2031.
  The totality inclination itself (89.2-89.95 deg) is weakly constrained, because R1/R2 is
  degenerate.
* Eclipse timing variations are very large and highly significant:
  - primary O-C spans -14 to +19 min (rms 12.5 min) against timing errors of 0.1-0.25 min;
  - the secondary-minus-primary interval shrinks by 27 min per cycle (2021) and 52 min per cycle
    (2023), but only ~4 min per cycle between seasons;
  - primary and secondary shifts are anti-correlated (an apsidal-type, short-period oscillation of
    omega).
* Interpretation: a third body on a fairly tight orbit is driving nodal precession of the
  21.8-d orbit (i(t)) and short-period perturbations of omega (the ETVs).

2. MODEL (ebmodel.py, ebfit.py, s08, s10)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Model setup:
  - Two limb-darkened spheres on an eccentric Keplerian orbit, solved exactly with Kepler's
    equation.
  - Occultation of each star uses the Mandel and Agol quadratic-LD routine from batman, which
    handles radius ratios > 1. It was verified against brute-force integration to 1e-6.
  - The degenerate z = |1-p| point that batman fails on is nudged.
  - Flux = [F1 + (J k^2) F2] / (1 + J k^2), then diluted by l3. Cadence integration is
    supersampled.

Parameters:
  - Shared across seasons: R1/a, R2/a, J (disk-averaged surface-brightness ratio, TESS band)
    and e sin w.
  - Per season: i (2021, 2023, 2026).
  - e cos w per season (2021, 2023), set iteratively from the season's mean secondary-minus-
    primary interval. e cos w for 2026 is tied to 2023, because no secondary was observed.
  - One free time shift per eclipse. This was required: with a linear ephemeris chi2 was
    114,700; with per-eclipse shifts it is 5,141, for N = 3757. The residuals were
    derivative-shaped, which signals the ETVs.
  - LD: quadratic (0.20, 0.30) for both stars, appropriate for ~7500-7750 K in the TESS band.
    J ~ 0.9 means both stars are of similar temperature.

Uncertainties:
  - Statistical: emcee, 48 walkers x 6000 steps, autocorrelation ~80 steps, half discarded as
    burn-in. Errors per eclipse are rescaled to the out-of-eclipse rms times a red-noise beta
    of 1.16-1.93.
  - Systematics: complete refits with
    - l3 = 0 and 0.15;
    - LD u1 and u2 each varied by +-0.1, and different LD for the two stars;
    - apertures of 1.5, 2.5 and 3.0 px;
    - the independent QLP reduction (2021 and 2023);
    - e sin w free per season.
  - Each systematic is the maximum |shift| within its group; the groups are added in quadrature.

Nominal solution (statistical errors only):
  R1/a = 0.03203 +- 0.00031     R2/a = 0.03708 +- 0.00026     J = 0.906 +- 0.0025
  e sin w = 0.2325 +- 0.0007    e cos w = -0.2593 (2021), -0.2658 (2023)
  e = 0.353, w = 138.8 deg (2023)
  - The sum (R1+R2)/a = 0.069 is robust across variants (0.068-0.070).
  - The split R1/R2 is not robust. Across variants R1 = 0.029-0.035 and R2 = 0.035-0.039. In
    the data-preferred l3 ~ -0.025 solution, R1 ~ R2.
  - A per-season e sin w improves chi2 by 486, for 1 extra parameter. w advances from
    137.7 deg (2021) to 139.5 deg (2023), i.e. +1.8 deg in 2.05 yr, with e constant at 0.350.
    The inclinations change by < 0.02 deg.

K2 2014 (s11):
  - Shape (R1, R2, e sin w) is taken from the TESS posterior; 300 posterior draws are
    propagated.
  - J is converted to the Kepler band with blackbody ratios: T2 = 7480 K for T1 = 7754 K, so
    J_Kp = 0.888. Kepler-band LD is (0.28, 0.30). e cos w (2014) = -0.235, extrapolated from
    2021-2023.
  - Free parameters: i2014, time shift and baseline.
  - Systematics: LD +-0.1; e cos w +-0.03; w extrapolated back with the 2021-2023 apsidal
    advance (w2014 = 131 deg, which gives i2014 = 85.60); and all TESS variants.

6. INTERPRETATION (Question 4)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Within one consistent photometric system, the eclipse depths and shapes from 2014 to 2026 are
reproduced by a single geometry. The only time-variable quantity needed is the orbital
inclination, which rises by ~2.8 deg in 11.7 yr (0.2 -> 0.31 deg/yr), plus a slow advance
of w.

The changing inclination of a detached 21.8-d binary, with e = 0.35, needs a torque on the
orbital plane. Each candidate explanation fares as follows:
  - Tides and GR apsidal motion cannot change i.
  - The rotational oblateness of stars with R/a ~ 0.035 is far too small for ~0.3 deg/yr of
    nodal precession.
  - Precession driven by a third body is the natural explanation, and it is supported by the
    ETVs:
    - O-C excursions of 10-20 min on single-cycle timescales;
    - primary and secondary shifts that are anti-correlated (an omega/e perturbation, not a
      light-travel-time delay);
    - a secondary-primary interval that swings by 27-52 min per cycle within a TESS season but
      changes little between seasons.
  These are the signatures of strong short-period dynamical perturbations, implying a fairly
  close tertiary (outer period plausibly of order 100-1000 d). The observed acceleration of
  di/dt is expected, because i(t) under nodal precession is not linear.
Predictions:
  - The eclipses keep deepening for the next ~4 yr.
  - The primary may become total around 2029-2031.
  - The ETVs and the varying secondary phase should be modelled jointly (photodynamically).
    That would yield the third body's period, mass and mutual inclination.

7. CAVEATS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- R1/a vs R2/a is degenerate with l3 and LD. This matters only for the totality epoch.
- e sin w is shared across seasons in the nominal fit. With it free per season, the
  inclinations move by <= 0.02 deg.
- The 2014 inclination relies on:
  - TESS-derived radii;
  - a blackbody band conversion of J;
  - e and w extrapolated to 2014.
  Its total systematic error (0.19 deg) is dominated by w(2014).
- The 2026 primary has a data gap during egress. The 2026 secondary is unobserved; 2026 e cos w
  is tied to 2023.
- The LD coefficients are approximate. They were not taken from a Claret table; they were
  varied by +-0.1.
- The models are Keplerian with per-eclipse time shifts; no photodynamical model was fitted.
- Within-season inclination and depth trends are not resolved: the scatter is ~0.01 deg and
  ~0.003 in depth.
