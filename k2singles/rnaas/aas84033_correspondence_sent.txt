Dear Dr Lintott,

Thank you for your decision on AAS82225, "Orbital Periods for K2 Single-transit and Single-eclipse Events from K2 Revisits and TESS", and for inviting a revision along the two lines you described.

1. A thorough search of the existing literature.

For each of the 163 stars I searched:
- ADS full text, under the star's EPIC, TIC, Gaia and 2MASS identifiers and any others SIMBAD lists;
- SIMBAD bibliographies;
- ExoFOP;
- VizieR: 22 variable-star, eclipsing-binary, K2 rotation and K2 planet-candidate catalogues for every star, plus 56 catalogues of the papers found for the stars in the table;
- the Gaia DR3 orbit catalogue;
- SPOC threshold-crossing events (TCEs) from Valizadegan et al. (2025) for every star, and the SPOC and TESS-SPOC data-validation reports on MAST for the stars in the table.

Each source for the 15 stars in the table was classified twice, independently: as giving a period, a period estimate, another kind of study, or only a catalogue listing. The two classifications disagreed on one period, and reading the paper settled it. A period is called new only if no source gives an orbital period. That rule was fixed before the sources were classified.

The result:
- 6 of the 15 periods are new. Five eclipsing binaries have no prior period, and EPIC 211503363 had only a period estimate.
- 5 match published periods to within 0.2%. The note cites them and explains why the other catalogue periods for these stars do not fit.
- 4 conflict with SPOC or TESS-SPOC TCEs: TOI-5560.01, EPIC 211489484, HD 220967 and EPIC 246089124. I checked every TCE epoch in the validation reports. The TCEs of the last two come from Sector 70 alone and each rest on one eclipse. The note explains why my data exclude each TCE, and does not call these four periods new.

The table is now grouped by these verdicts. The note also states the search's limits:
- ADS and SIMBAD alone find only a third of the known star-paper pairs, because catalogue papers often name stars only in machine-readable tables. That is why VizieR was searched.
- The VizieR catalogue list was fixed after an all-catalogue query failed, because the mirror's database was unavailable.
- 13 of the 93 listed catalogues were not on the mirror.
- One known paper lists a star just outside the 3" search radius.

2. A blind search for transits.

The submitted note slid a box with the catalogued depth and duration along the data. The revised note keeps those periods, but tests them with a blind search of all 163 stars:
- no sample cut, and no use of the catalogued depth, duration or epoch;
- every K2 light curve (three reductions, all campaigns) and the best TESS pipeline light curve per sector, with my own photometry for recent sectors. This covers 364 of 447 TESS target-sectors; the other 83 have no pipeline light curve and were not searched;
- thresholds from the same search run on inverted light curves.

For 11 of the 15 systems, the reported period is among the blind search's allowed periods. The note says plainly that the four misses include both planet candidates, and explains the other two. The blind search's first stage does find every transit of both candidates (SNR 9-32 and 11-111). Six of the seven fall below the significance threshold, which is high because inverted brightenings such as glints and bad cadences inflate the control. A linked-event test added after the first full run is labelled as post hoc. It recovers one of the four misses, and the note explains why its result for TOI-5560.01 is not a detection.

Blind signals on 47 other stars are listed in the repository and are not claimed. A first triage of them is under way in the repository; its results will be reported separately.

Other changes. The title now names the blind search. The audit of the catalogued events is replaced by the blind recovery. Some descriptive detail was cut to stay within the word limit.

The search rules were fixed before the full run; the detection threshold was set after a one-star test. After the full run, a coincidence test replaced the planned rule for systematics shared between stars, and a linked-event test was added. Every such change is dated and explained in the repository (k2singles/revision2/PLAN.md), and every number in the note traces to a script there (https://github.com/coracle-us/duo-transits).
