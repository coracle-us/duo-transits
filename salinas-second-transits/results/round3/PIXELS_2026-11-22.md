# Pixel-level and Gaia vetting of three periodic stars, 2026-11-22

Script: `round3/s12_pixels.py`. Tables: `pixel_summary.csv`, plus `tic*_pixel_check.csv` and
`tic*_gaia_neighbours.csv` for each star; images in `tic*_pixel_check.png`.

This is the check round 2 §5 ran on TIC 230133616, now applied to the other three stars that round 2
flagged as periodic. It covers every clean transit in `tic*_transits.csv`, with mid-times from the
round 3 ephemeris.

For each transit, the script measures:
- a 15x15 TESScut difference image (out of transit minus in transit) and its flux-weighted centroid
  within 3 px of the target;
- the brightest difference pixel anywhere in the cutout;
- the aperture's in- minus out-of-transit centroid shift.

The centroid offsets are converted to sky coordinates and averaged over transits. The error comes from
the scatter between transits.

The ″ offsets are 0.10–0.18 TESS pixels, the same range as 230133616 in round 2 (1.9–3.9″).
They are formally about 3σ from the target. That is the usual systematic floor of this crude centroid
(flux-weighted, positive pixels only), not a shift towards any star.

A source within 2′ counts as able to make the dip when it would need less than 100% of its own flux.
- **275647095:** 23 such sources; the nearest two are 28σ and 36σ from the centroid.
- **241550976:** 36 such sources.
- **107113048:** 11 such sources, none closer than 47″.

Every difference image is centred on the target, with two exceptions:
- **107113048, S90 (epoch 29):** the difference image is dominated by a bright star about 5 px east.
  - The aperture centroid jumps 16.5 mpix there, against 1–4 mpix for the other transits.
  - The TESScut aperture depth is twice that of the other transits.
  - This is a pointing or scattered-light excursion during that window. The script flags any transit
    whose shift is over 3× the star's median, and leaves it out of the stack. With it, the offset is
    6.5 ± 3.3″.
  - The transit itself is real: it is in QLP at 9,100 ppm, and round 3 timed it on ephemeris.
- **275647095, S64 (epochs 43 and 44):** the brightest difference pixel is a ± pattern on a bright star
  7 px away (pointing jitter). The target pixel itself shows the dip, and those two local centroids are
  within 1.2″ of the target.

None of the three shows a sign of a resolved or astrometric companion.

For stars this faint, DR3 gives the median of the epoch RVs, with error √(π/2N) × their scatter. That
gives the epoch scatter above, which includes the measurement noise.

A rough companion-mass bound: a companion of 0.1 M☉ would give K ≈ 8.5, 7.1 and 5.8 km/s at these
periods, which is a scatter of about K/√2.
- **275647095:** its 3.2 km/s scatter argues against a companion above about 0.1 M☉.
- **The other two:** a companion above about 0.2 M☉ would be needed to exceed their scatter.

This is only indicative. Gaia DR4 (2 Dec 2026) publishes the epoch RVs, which will test this
directly.

## What it leaves open

- **A companion within about 1″ (an unresolved blend or a bound eclipsing binary) is not excluded.** It
  would also have to keep the odd/even depths and the phase-0.5 flux flat (round 3).
- **275647095's low transit density (0.22 of the TIC value) is still unexplained.** The signal is on
  target, so that leaves an eccentric orbit, a star larger than the TIC radius, or a companion within
  about 1″.
- **TESScut aperture depths** (medians 7,600, 11,800 and 5,000 ppm) run under QLP's. This is the dilution
  noted in round 3; they are used here only to confirm the dip is in the aperture.
