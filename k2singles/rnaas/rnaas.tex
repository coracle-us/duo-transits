\title{Orbital Periods for K2 Single-transit and Single-eclipse Events from K2 Revisits and TESS, Checked by a Blind Search}

\author[orcid=0009-0000-7524-4470]{Jon McBee}
\affiliation{Independent researcher, USA}
\email{jon.mcbee@gmail.com}

\begin{abstract}
\citet{LaCourse2018} catalogued 164 K2 events seen only once. I report periods for 15 of them from
repeats in later K2 campaigns and TESS, and test them with a blind search of all 163 stars, with
thresholds from the same search on inverted light curves. The blind search finds 11 of the 15
periods among its allowed periods; it misses both planet candidates. A search of the literature,
VizieR catalogues and SPOC validation products finds no prior orbital period for six.
TOI-5560.01 narrows from $\leq702$\,d to $140.494$ or $70.247$\,d, excluding four SPOC
threshold-crossing events. The K2 candidate EPIC~211503363 has $P=122.905$ or $61.453$\,d, but
its companion is $\approx2.1\,R_{\rm J}$, probably an M dwarf. The primary eclipse of
EPIC~202126877 deepened from 6\% in 2014 to 36\% in 2026.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{\uat{Exoplanet detection methods}{489} --- \uat{Transit photometry}{1709} --- \uat{Eclipsing binary stars}{444}}

\section{Methods}

\textit{Periods.} Boxes of catalogued depth and duration were matched to repeats in K2 PDCSAP
\citep{Howell2014}, TESS SPOC, TESS-SPOC \citep{Caldwell2020}, QLP \citep{Huang2020} and my TESScut
\citep{Brasseur2019} light curves. Trapezoid fits give linear ephemerides, which independent
re-analyses match within $2\sigma$ (EPIC~202126877 has timing variations).

\textit{Blind search.} All 163 stars were searched, ignoring catalogued depths, durations and epochs,
in every K2 light curve (PDCSAP, EVEREST, K2SFF), the best TESS pipeline light curve per sector and my
photometry for Sectors $\geq80$: 364 of 447 TESS target-sectors (the other 83, faint stars before
Sector 80, lack pipeline data). Dips of 1--48\,h are fitted against a local baseline; the same search
of inverted light curves sets the threshold at 0.05 false events per star (SNR 53 in K2, 60 in TESS).
Significant events of similar depth and duration are linked as $P=\Delta T/n$, rejecting periods with
an epoch on flat data. Box least-squares (BLS) searches of each data block (periods up to half its
span, at most 100\,d) are thresholded at the 99th percentile of inverted blocks' maximum power. The
threshold followed a one-star test; a coincidence test for shared systematics (replacing the planned
rule) and a linked-event test calibrated on inverted data came after the full run.

\textit{Literature.} I searched ADS full text under each star's SIMBAD identifiers, SIMBAD
bibliographies, ExoFOP, 22 variable-star and K2 VizieR catalogues plus 56 from papers on
Table~\ref{tab:k2} stars, Gaia DR3 orbits \citep{Arenou2023}, and SPOC and TESS-SPOC TCEs
\citep{Jenkins2016,Twicken2018,Valizadegan2025}. Two independent classifications of the 203 sources
disagreed once (settled by reading it). A period is new only if no source gives one. Limits:
ADS and SIMBAD found 3 of 9 known (star, paper) pairs; the VizieR list's recall is unmeasurable; 13 of
93 listed catalogues were missing from the mirror; the 3$''$ radius misses the 4.4$''$
\citet{Libralato2016} entry for EPIC~211892898.

\section{Results}

The blind search finds a first-stage event (SNR $\geq8$) at 163 of 164 catalogued epochs and a
significant one for 112 stars (with the post-hoc test). It recovers 11 of the 15 periods, missing both
planet candidates (six of their seven transits are below threshold) and two eccentric binaries whose
eclipses it merges; the post-hoc test recovers EPIC~211489484. It links all four TOI-5560.01 transits
at 140.49\,d, but only at threshold support, among 16 periods and below the star's inverted control:
not a detection. Another 47 stars show blind signals (36 of 470 BLS blocks pass, against 5 inverted);
triage leaves no on-target candidate. Code and outputs:
\url{https://github.com/coracle-us/duo-transits}.

Most of the 15 systems are eclipsing binaries. Six have no prior orbital period, five match a
published one within 0.2\%, and four conflict with SPOC TCEs that my data exclude. Differing catalogue
periods for the C0 binaries (VSX 3.84--8.70\,d; 5.6--1008\,d for EPIC~202072917) miss consecutive
observed eclipses by $\geq0.16$\,d.

\textit{TOI-5560.01 (EPIC~211924561).} Sectors 46 and 72 left $P\leq702.47$\,d. The K2 C5 and C16
transits share their linear ephemeris ($T_0={\rm BJD_{TDB}}\,2457179.8979\pm0.0014$), and flat data
exclude all its submultiples but half (Table~\ref{tab:k2}). SPOC TCEs at 58.28, 31.25, 58.54 and
10.28\,d rely on a scattered-light step at BJD~2459509.8 or on dip-free times, and none fits the C5
transit. The V-shaped 3.0-h, 2.3-ppt transit is far shorter than a central one (12--15\,h): the orbit
is grazing, eccentric or both. Both periods predict transits at ${\rm BJD_{TDB}}\,2461394.716\pm0.004$
(2026 December 20) and $2461535.210\pm0.005$ (2027 May 9); one at 2461464.963 (2027 February 28) would
confirm 70.25\,d.

\textit{EPIC~211503363.} This single-transit candidate of \citet{Kovacs2020} ($P\gtrsim58$\,d)
transits again in Sectors 45 and 72 (3.4--3.8\,ppt, 23\,h; Table~\ref{tab:k2}). With
$R_\star=3.6\,R_\odot$ ($\log g=3.46$; Gaia DR3) the companion is $\approx2.1\,R_{\rm J}$
($2.4\,R_{\rm J}$ with Kov\'acs's $R_\star$), probably an M dwarf.

\textit{Eclipsing binaries.} HD~220967's eclipses are 37\% and 30\% deep; the phase-0.867 secondary
gives $e\geq0.58$. TESS-SPOC TCEs of HD~220967 (4.47\,d) and EPIC~246089124 (9.54\,d) each rest on
one Sector~70 eclipse, with flat other epochs. EPIC~211489484's 106.94-d TCE joins two primaries ten
cycles apart, excluded by four others; its 0.24-d TCE is five cycles of a $\delta$~Scuti-type
pulsation (20.79\,d$^{-1}$), probably from a neighbour 26$''$ away.
