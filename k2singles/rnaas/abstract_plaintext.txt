LaCourse & Jacobs (2018) catalogued 164 K2 events seen only once. We searched all later K2 campaigns and all TESS data for repeats of the 144 events that TESS should detect. Every period we report comes from fitted eclipse times and was re-derived blind with independent code. We link K2 C5 and C16 transits to the TESS transits of TOI-5560.01, narrowing its period from at most 702 d to 140.4939 or 70.2470 d. The K2 planet candidate EPIC 211503363 has P = 122.905 or 61.453 d, but its companion is about 2.2 Jupiter radii, probably an M dwarf. We give new or refined periods or short alias lists for nine eclipsing binaries. These include HD 220967, a bright, eccentric (e >= 0.58), 73.245-d system. In another, EPIC 202126877, the primary eclipse deepened from 6% in 2014 to 20% in 2021 and 32% in 2025.
