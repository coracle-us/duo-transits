KEPLER SINGLE/DUO TRANSITS + TESS REPEATS: SCOPING REPORT
Date: 2026-10-12. Everything lives in kepler_singles_scoping/. Nothing outside it was modified. Nothing is committed.

VERDICT: NO-GO for a full run.
- Expected yield: about 1 TESS repeat among planet-like Kepler singles in the data so far, and a repeat would
  typically leave about 4 aliases, not a unique period.
- Duplication risk is low; the problem is yield.
- Kepler singles have P_min of about 600 d, because the Kepler data are near-continuous over 4 yr. TESS has
  covered each Kepler-field star for only about 9 sectors.
- Compare K2: 50 targets gave 15 repeats. Here about 28 SNR-capable singles give about 3 repeats, two-thirds of
  them on eclipsing-binary-like objects.

1. CATALOGUES OF KEPLER SINGLE / LONG-PERIOD TRANSIT CANDIDATES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Parsed: scripts/parse_catalogs.py writes data/events_by_source.csv.
Merged: scripts/build_targets.py writes data/kepler_singles_targets.csv.
Raw arXiv LaTeX sources and VizieR tables are in data/raw/.

[KM19] Kawahara & Masuda 2019, AJ 157, 218; arXiv:1904.04980.
  - Size: the largest catalogue, 67 "clean" plus 23 "flagged" candidates (grazing, too large, or giant host),
    on 89 stars. About 26 have two Kepler transits.
  - Columns: KIC, KOI, Kp, M*, R*, Rp, t0 (BKJD), P posterior, b, e. No depth or duration column; we derive
    depth = (Rp/R*)^2 and a circular-orbit T14.
  - Data: VizieR J/AJ/157/218 (table2 is stellar parameters; table3 is the 67 clean candidates). The flagged
    table is only in the arXiv source (table_rm.tex).
  - Coverage: it largely subsumes W15, U16 and FM16 (the dagger marks in its table).

[W15] Wang et al. 2015, ApJ 815, 127; Planet Hunters VIII; arXiv:1512.02559.
  - Size: 41 candidates on 38 stars: 17 with one transit, 14 with two, 10 with three or more.
  - Columns: KIC, KOI, P (mode and range), a/R*, i, Rp/R*, epoch (BKJD).
  - Data: in the arXiv source (orbital_params_1/2/3.tex). Not in VizieR (J/ApJ/815/127 does not exist).

[FM16] Foreman-Mackey et al. 2016, AJ 152, 206; arXiv:1607.08237.
  - Size: 16 candidates (13 single, 3 double). Automated search of about 40,000 bright GK dwarfs.
  - Columns: KIC, Teff, R*, Kp, P (years), t0 (KBJD), Rp, duration (h), b.
  - Data: Table "catalog" in the arXiv source (ms.tex).

[HZW19] Herman, Zhu & Wu 2019, AJ, doi:10.3847/1538-3881/ab1f70; arXiv:1901.01974.
  - Size: 19 candidates, 15 deemed genuine. The FM16 pipeline was re-run on a larger sample with Gaia DR2
    radii, which demoted several FM16 candidates.
  - Data: in the arXiv source (param_tab.tex).
  - Journal volume and page not verified; quoted as AJ 157, 248 elsewhere.

[U16] Uehara et al. 2016, ApJ 822, 2; arXiv:1602.07848.
  - Size: 28 single-transit events on 24 KOI stars, found by eye in 7,557 KOI light curves.
  - Columns: KIC, KOI, Kp, Tc (KBJD), depth (ppm), Teff, logg, P_min. MCMC durations for 16 events.
  - Data: in the arXiv source (main.tex, tables tab:list and tab:list_fit).

[HC24] Hansen & Dittmann 2024, arXiv:2403.03427.
  - Size: 1 machine-learning single, KOI 1271.02 (KIC 8631160), at about BKJD 679, Rp 5.3 Re.
  - Data: values entered by hand from the text.

Found but not parsed:
  - Hansen & Dittmann 2026, arXiv:2608.23425: 4 new long-period candidates, 2 of them singles (Kepler 1897.02 and
    1811.02). PDF only.
  - Priyanshu 2026, "EXOVEIL", arXiv:2606.02778: 46 monotransit "transit-like anomalies". Single author, machine
    learning, reframed in v3 as anomalies for follow-up. It recovers 32% of transits at 1000 ppm, so its
    candidates are mostly far too shallow for TESS on Kp 13–15 stars.
  - Ivashtenko & Zackay 2025, arXiv:2501.03019: a pipeline paper. About 50 high-probability long-period events
    were promised for a later catalogue, which is not yet published.
  - Schmitt et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.09229: Kepler-150 f. It is already in KM19 (KIC 5351250, a duo).
  - Cui et al. 2021: a machine-learning single-transit search (not verified).
  - Quinn et al. 2021, arXiv:2107.00027: KIC 4918810, a single-transit substellar object, P of about 10 yr from
    RVs. It is in KM19.

The Kepler KOI tables are not a source.
  - Files: NASA Exoplanet Archive cumulative and q1_q17_dr25_koi tables, in data/raw/koi_*.csv.
  - The KOI pipeline (TPS) needs at least 3 transits.
  - koi_num_transits ≤ 2 matches 431 rows, but these are almost all false positives with junk num_transits = 0
    (14 are CANDIDATE).
  - KOIs with exactly 3 transits (181 rows) already have periods.
  - The long-period singles enter the KOI list only through the papers above (for example, U16 "CS" rows such
    as KOI-99.01 and 1096.01).

Merged list:
  - 186 source rows give 111 unique candidates on 102 stars: 81 singles and 30 duos.
  - KIC → TIC matching used the TIC's KIC column: 111 of 111 matched.

2. PRIOR TESS-ERA AND OTHER FOLLOW-UP (duplication risk)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Literature search by a sub-agent, 2026-10-12, using arXiv and NASA Exoplanet Archive queries. No ADS full-text
search was available.

No paper systematically searches TESS for repeats of Kepler single or duo transits.

Closest work, all on planets whose periods were already known:
  - Christ, Montet & Fabrycky 2019 (AJ 157, 235; arXiv:1810.02826): predictions of which KOIs TESS will recover.
  - Battley et al. 2021 (MNRAS 503, 4092; arXiv:2103.03259): ephemerides from S14/15/26.
  - Jontof-Hutter, Dalba & Livingston 2022 (AJ 164, 42; arXiv:2207.08917): TTV systems in S14–S41.
  - Kaye & Aigrain 2025 (MNRAS 538, 2283; doi:10.1093/mnras/staf334): 111 Kepler and K2 planets with known
    periods, TESS to S82. The closest paper; it must be cited. arXiv ID not found.
  - Yahalomi et al. 2023 (arXiv:2310.03802): Kepler-1513 b.
  - Perrocheau et al. 2022 (arXiv:2211.01532): Kepler-167 e from the ground; TESS missed it.

Explicit TESS check of a Kepler single:
  - Hansen & Dittmann 2024 (arXiv:2403.03427), KOI 1271.02. Sectors 14, 15, 40, 41, 54, 55 and 74 inspected; no
    transit found.
  - Our predicted SNR for this candidate is below 7 anyway.

Single-transit and long-period follow-up by other means (RV, Spitzer, ground):
  - GOT 'EM survey: I (arXiv:2012.04676), II (Kepler-1704 b, arXiv:2107.06901), IV (arXiv:2401.03021), V
    (arXiv:2405.11017).
  - GOT 'EM IV: KIC 5951458 has an RV period of about 3378 d and is likely an M-dwarf companion.
  - Dalba et al. 2020 (arXiv:2008.02811): KIC 5951458.
  - Dalba & Muirhead 2016 (arXiv:1606.09246): Kepler-421 b.
  - Dalba & Tamburo 2019: Spitzer, Kepler-167 e.
  - Quinn et al. 2021: KIC 4918810.
  - Santerne et al. 2016 (SOPHIE): a high false-positive rate among Kepler giant candidates. Use this as the
    vetting prior.

Operational risk, which is the real one:
  - SPOC/QLP have been issuing single-transit TOIs on long-period Kepler hosts through 2026, for example
    TOI 7068 (Kepler-1514 b), 7859 (PH-2 b), 7906 and 7909.
  - TSTPC and Planet Hunters TESS post Kepler-field singles as CTOIs.
  - None of our 102 stars has a TOI or CTOI in the repo's ExoFOP tables of 2026-10-11 (data/feasibility.csv,
    TOI and CTOI columns).
  - Re-check the live tables before any claim.

Already solved or closed in our list (column lit_note):
  - Duos with known periods: Kepler-167 e, 421 b, 1654 b, 1708 b, 1704 b and 150 f.
  - KIC 5951458: RV result, see above.
  - KIC 4918810: substellar.
  - KOI 1271.02: already checked against TESS.
  - 28 of 30 duos already have a unique period from Kepler coverage alone, so a TESS repeat would only refine
    the ephemeris.

3. FEASIBILITY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scripts: scripts/tess_products_and_noise.py, scripts/feasibility.py and scripts/work_estimate.py.
Outputs: data/noise_model.txt, data/feasibility.csv, data/feasibility_summary.txt and data/work_estimate.txt.

TESS coverage (tess-point 0.9, max_sector 134):
  - All 102 stars are observed.
  - Sectors per star: median 9, range 6–17. That is 976 target-sectors through S82, plus a few in S56, S73, S76
    and S83.
  - Sectors seen: S14, 15, 26, 40, 41, 53, 54, 55, 74, 75, 80, 81 and 82.
  - No Kepler-field sectors fall in S84–S111.
  - Future pointings in tess-point: S118–121, S125–127 and S132 (2027–28).

Pipeline light curves, from the MAST search (data/tess_lc_inventory.csv):
  - Stars covered: SPOC 2-min for 50, TESS-SPOC for 53, QLP for 49 and TASOC for 57.
  - 18 of the 102 stars have no TESS light-curve product at all.

Noise model (empirical) and SNR:
  - Method: 132 TESS-SPOC/QLP/SPOC sector light curves of these stars, flattened over 1 d and binned to 1 h,
    scatter measured as MAD.
  - Fit: log10 sigma_1h[ppm] = 3.214 + 0.308 (Tmag − 13), i.e. 800 / 1600 / 3300 / 6800 ppm at Tmag 12 / 13 /
    14 / 15. There is no difference between the S14–55 and S56+ eras.
  - Comparison: the old model 220 ppm × 10^(0.2 (T − 9.1)) agrees at T = 12 and is too optimistic by 1.6× at
    T = 14 and 2× at T = 15.
  - SNR per transit = depth / sigma_1h × sqrt(duration), with duration capped at 30 h.
  - SNR ≥ 7 holds for 37 of 111 candidates (52 with the old model). Of those, 15 are planet-like and 22 are
    eclipsing-binary-like.
  - "Planet-like" means not in the KM19 flagged table, depth ≤ 2.5%, and not tagged as an EB.

Repeat probability:
  - Kepler coverage: the quarters each star was actually observed (MAST), using approximate quarter windows.
  - Singles: Monte Carlo over P with prior dN/dlnP ∝ P^(-2/3) on 50–7300 d. Periods that would put another
    transit in Kepler data are rejected.
  - p_repeat is the fraction of the remaining prior with a transit centre in TESS data, times 0.85 for usable
    data. A version using the catalogues' own P values (factor-2 lognormal) gives the same totals within 5%.
  - Duos: aliases dT/n surviving the Kepler coverage, weighted by P^(-2/3).

  |----------------------------------------------|----|------------------|

  - After a simulated repeat, the Kepler and TESS flat-data cuts leave a median of 4 aliases, range 2–9.
  - So a repeat would narrow the period, not resolve it. Resolving it would need a third observed transit.

Work estimate for a full run:
  - All SNR ≥ 7 singles: 26 stars, 245 target-sectors. 140 have a SPOC, TESS-SPOC or QLP light curve and 105
    need our own TESScut photometry. 7 stars have no pipeline light curve at all.
  - Planet-like subset: 11 stars, 100 target-sectors, 25 of them TESScut only.
  - The k2singles pipeline (singles/scripts/pilot.py with the Kepler epoch as reference) would carry over
    almost unchanged. Estimated cost: about 1 day of compute and checking, plus vetting.

4. RECOMMENDATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NO-GO for a full run as a research product.

Expected new periods:
  - Planet-like: about 1 TESS repeat expected (Poisson chance of zero is about 33%).
  - That repeat would leave about 4 aliases, and it still has to survive vetting. Kepler giant singles have a
    high false-positive rate, and in K2, 15 repeats yielded 1 planet-like lead.
  - Realistically: 0–1 "narrowed to a few aliases" planet candidate, and about 0.1–0.2 uniquely resolved.
  - EB-like singles: 1–2 stellar-companion periods (2.2 repeats expected among 17), as in the K2 note, also
    with several aliases each.

Duplication risk: low in the literature. The main risks are operational (SPOC/QLP single-transit TOIs and
TSTPC/PHT CTOIs in the Kepler field) and the long-period planets already solved by RV or duos (listed in §2).

Work: small (26 stars, 245 target-sectors, 105 needing TESScut). Cost is not the obstacle; yield is.

Best starting catalogue if pursued: Kawahara & Masuda 2019 (VizieR J/AJ/157/218 plus the flagged table).
  - It is homogeneous, machine-readable and the largest.
  - It holds 0.78 of the 1.1 expected planet-like repeats.
  - Add the three U16-only events (KIC 2162635, 1717722 and 4042088).
  - W15, FM16 and HZW19 add nothing new at SNR ≥ 7.

If anything is done:
  - A time-boxed quick look (at most half a day) at the 11 planet-like and 15 EB-like SNR ≥ 7 singles, using
    existing pipeline light curves only.
  - Or revisit after the 2027–28 Kepler-field sectors (S118–S132), which raise the expectation by about 50%.
  - Neither is likely to justify an RNAAS on its own. At most it would make a paragraph or a combined note with
    the K2 work.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAVEATS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Kepler coverage uses approximate quarter boundaries and ignores the monthly downlink gaps. For 6 duos every
  alias was "rejected", so the catalogue period n = 1 was kept (column coverage_note).
- Depths ignore limb darkening. Durations are partly computed from circular orbits.
- The noise model was measured on stars that have pipeline light curves; fainter stars needing TESScut will
  likely be worse.
- Future sector times (S112+) are approximated as contiguous 27.4-d sectors.
- The sub-agent's literature search had no ADS full-text access. Kaye & Aigrain 2025's tables were not read.
- Housekeeping: data/lk_cache/ (about 82 MB of TESS FITS) and data/raw/ (about 51 MB of arXiv sources and KOI
  tables) are not gitignored. Exclude them before any commit.
