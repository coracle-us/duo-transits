\title{Orbital Periods for K2 Single-transit and Single-eclipse Events from K2 Revisits and TESS, Checked by a Blind Search}

\author[orcid=0009-0000-7524-4470]{Jon McBee}
\affiliation{Independent researcher, USA}
\email{jon.mcbee@gmail.com}

\begin{abstract}
\citet{LaCourse2018} catalogued 164 K2 events seen only once. I report periods for 15 of them from
repeats in later K2 campaigns and TESS, and test them with a blind search of all 163 stars, with
thresholds from the same search on inverted light curves. The blind search finds 11 of the 15
periods among its allowed periods; it misses both planet candidates. A search of the literature,
VizieR catalogues and SPOC validation products finds no prior orbital period for six.
TOI-5560.01 narrows from $\leq702$\,d to $140.494$ or $70.247$\,d, excluding four SPOC
threshold-crossing events. The K2 candidate EPIC~211503363 has $P=122.905$ or $61.453$\,d, but
its companion is $\approx2.1\,R_{\rm J}$, probably an M dwarf. The primary eclipse of
EPIC~202126877 deepened from 6\% in 2014 to 36\% in 2026.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{\uat{Exoplanet detection methods}{489} --- \uat{Transit photometry}{1709} --- \uat{Eclipsing binary stars}{444}}

\section{Methods}

\textit{Periods.} A box of the catalogued depth and
duration was matched to repeats in K2 PDCSAP \citep{Howell2014}, TESS SPOC, TESS-SPOC
\citep{Caldwell2020} and QLP \citep{Huang2020} light curves and my own TESScut photometry
\citep{Brasseur2019}. Final periods come from trapezoid fits to each eclipse and a linear
ephemeris. Independent re-analyses agree within $2\sigma$
(EPIC~202126877 shows timing variations).

\textit{Blind search.} I then searched all 163 stars, with no sample cut and no use of the
catalogued depth, duration or epoch: every K2 light curve (PDCSAP, EVEREST, K2SFF; all campaigns)
and the best TESS pipeline light curve per sector, plus my own photometry for Sectors $\geq80$. This covers 364 of
447 TESS target-sectors; the other 83, of faint stars before Sector 80, have no pipeline light
curve. Dips of 1--48\,h are fitted against a local baseline. The identical search on inverted light
curves sets the threshold at 0.05 false events per star (SNR 53 in K2, 60 in TESS). Significant
events of similar depth and duration on one star are linked as $P=\Delta T/n$, rejecting any period
with a predicted epoch on flat data. A box least-squares search of each data block (periods up to
the shorter of half its span and 100\,d) is thresholded at the 99th percentile of inverted blocks' maximum power.
The rules were fixed before the full run, the threshold after a one-star test. After it, a
coincidence test replaced the planned rule for systematics shared between stars, and a linked-event
test calibrated on inverted data was added.

\textit{Literature.} For each star I searched ADS full text under its EPIC, TIC, Gaia and 2MASS
identifiers and any others in SIMBAD; SIMBAD bibliographies; ExoFOP; 22 variable-star and K2 VizieR
catalogues, plus 56 from papers on Table~\ref{tab:k2} stars; Gaia DR3 orbits
\citep{Arenou2023}; and SPOC and TESS-SPOC TCEs \citep{Jenkins2016,Twicken2018,Valizadegan2025}. Two independent classifications of
the 203 sources for Table~\ref{tab:k2} stars disagreed once; reading the paper
settled it. A period is new only if no source gives an orbital period. Gaps: ADS and SIMBAD found 3 of
9 known (star, paper) pairs, as catalogue papers often name stars only in tables; the VizieR list,
chosen after an all-catalogue query failed, includes those papers' catalogues, so cannot measure
recall; 13 of 93
listed catalogues were not on the mirror; and the 3$''$ radius misses the 4.4$''$ entry of
\citet{Libralato2016} for EPIC~211892898.

\section{Results}

The blind search finds a first-stage event (SNR $\geq8$) at 163 of the 164 catalogued epochs, and a
significant one for 112 stars (with the post-hoc coincidence test). For 11 of the 15
Table~\ref{tab:k2} systems, my period is among the blind search's allowed periods. It misses both planet candidates, six of whose seven transits fall below the threshold, and two eccentric binaries whose
primaries and secondaries it groups as one family. The post-hoc test recovers EPIC~211489484. For
TOI-5560.01 it links all four transits at 140.49\,d, but only at threshold support, among 16
periods and below that star's inverted control, so this is not a detection. Another 47 stars show
blind signals (36 of 470 box least-squares blocks pass, against 5 inverted ones), listed in the
repository, unvetted.

Of the 15 periods, six have no prior orbital period, five match a
published period within 0.2\%, and four conflict with SPOC TCEs that my data exclude. Catalogue periods
for the C0 binaries that differ from mine (VSX 3.84--8.70\,d; 5.6--1008\,d for EPIC~202072917)
miss consecutive observed eclipses by $\geq0.16$\,d.

\textit{TOI-5560.01 (EPIC~211924561).} Transits in TESS Sectors 46 and 72 left $P\leq702.47$\,d.
The K2 C5 and C16 transits fit one linear ephemeris with them, and flat data exclude all its submultiples
except half: $P=140.49392\pm0.00014$ or $70.24696\pm0.00007$\,d,
$T_0={\rm BJD_{TDB}}\,2457179.8979\pm0.0014$. SPOC TCEs at 58.28, 31.25, 58.54 and 10.28\,d each
include a scattered-light step at BJD~2459509.8 or times without a transit-depth dip, and none fits
the C5 transit. The V-shaped 3.0-h transit (2.3\,ppt) is far shorter than a central one
(12--15\,h), so the orbit is grazing, eccentric or both. Both periods predict
transits at ${\rm BJD_{TDB}}\,2461394.716\pm0.004$ (2026 December 20) and $2461535.210\pm0.005$
(2027 May 9); one at 2461464.963 (2027 February 28) would confirm 70.25\,d.

\textit{EPIC~211503363.} This single-transit candidate of \citet{Kovacs2020} ($P\gtrsim58$\,d)
transits again in Sectors 45 and 72 (3.4--3.8\,ppt, 23\,h): $P=122.9052\pm0.0006$ or
$61.4526\pm0.0003$\,d. With $R_\star=3.6\,R_\odot$ ($\log g=3.46$; Gaia DR3) the companion is
$\approx2.1\,R_{\rm J}$ ($2.4\,R_{\rm J}$ with Kov\'acs's $R_\star$), probably an M dwarf.

\textit{Eclipsing binaries.} HD~220967 shows eclipses 37\% and 30\% deep; the secondary
at phase 0.867 gives $e\geq0.58$. TESS-SPOC TCEs of HD~220967 (4.47\,d) and EPIC~246089124
(9.54\,d) each rest on one Sector~70 eclipse; their other epochs are flat. For EPIC~211489484 the 106.94-d TCE
joins two primaries ten cycles apart, and four other primaries exclude it. Its 0.24-d TCE is five
cycles of a $\delta$~Scuti-type pulsation (20.79\,d$^{-1}$), probably of a neighbour 26$''$ away.

EPIC~202126877 \citep{IJspeert2024} was 6.2\% deep in K2, with no secondary. In my fixed-aperture
TESS photometry the primary is 21\% deep in 2021, 27\% in 2023 and 36\% in January 2026, and the
secondary grows from 4\% to 12\% (2021--2023). The
secondary-to-primary ratio doubles, which constant dilution cannot do; the orbit may be
precessing.

\section{Conclusion}

Most K2 single events with repeats are eclipsing binaries, but repeats narrow a TESS planet
candidate to two periods and show a K2 planet candidate is probably an M dwarf. Code, fits,
literature tables and unvetted signals: \url{https://github.com/coracle-us/duo-transits}.
